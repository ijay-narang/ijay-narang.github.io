Research Direction

As AI becomes more capable of solving difficult problems, I see the fundamental value of research shifting toward asking the right questions. This means thinking carefully about what is worth studying, which assumptions deserve closer examination, and what a solution can teach us beyond the problem itself.

Theoretical computer science, statistical physics, and convex optimization offer many tools and ways of understanding problems that remain underused in practice. My goal is to explore connections between these fields and develop new techniques for both theoretically motivated questions and practical challenges in learning, inference, and system design. For example, when are natural formulations of practical problems NP-hard, and what does that imply for algorithm design? How does the balance between energy and entropy affect the difficulty of sampling, and can we use this understanding to provide useful heuristics?
